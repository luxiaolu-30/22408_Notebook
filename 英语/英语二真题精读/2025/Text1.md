U.S. customers historically tipped 给小费 people they assumed were earning most of their income via tips, such as restaurant servers earning less than the minimum wage 最低工资. In the early 2010s, a wide range of businesses started processing purchases with iPads and other digital payment systems 数字支付系统. These systems often prompted 提示 customers to tip for services that were not previously tipped. Today's tip requests are not connected to the salary and service norms 规范 that used to determine when and how people tip. Customers in the past merely always paid tips after receiving service, such as at the conclusion 结束 of a restaurant meal, after getting a haircut or once a pizza was delivered. That timing could reward high-quality service and give workers an incentive 激励 to provide it. It's becoming more common for tips to be requested beforehand 事先. And now tipping technology may even automatically add tips.

The prevalence 普及 of digital payment devices has made it easier to ask customers for a tip. That helps explain why tip requests are creeping into 悄悄进入 new kinds of services. Customers now routinely see menus of suggested default options 默认选项—often well above 20% of what they owe. The amounts have risen from 10% or less in the 1950s to 15% around the year 2000 to 20% or higher today. This increase is sometimes called tipflation 小费通胀—the expectation of ever-higher tip amounts. Tipping has always been a vital source of income for workers in historically tipped services, like restaurants, where the tipped minimum wage can be as low as US $2.13 an hour. Tip creep 小费蔓延 and tipflation are now further supplementing 补充 the income of many low-wage service workers.

Notably, tipping primarily benefits some of these workers, such as servers, but not others, such as cooks and dishwashers 洗碗机工, so to ensure that all employees were paid fair wages. Some restaurants banned tipping and increased prices, but the movement toward no-tipping services has largely fizzled out 失败.

So, to increase employee wages without raising prices, more employers are succumbing to 屈服于 the temptations of tip creep and tipflation. However, many customers are frustrated 沮丧 because they feel they are being asked for too high of a tip too often. And, as our research emphasizes, tipping now seems to be more coercive 强制性的, less generous, and often completely disassociated from 脱离 service quality.

## 长难句1

> U.S. customers historically tipped people they assumed were earning most of their income via tips, such as restaurant servers earning less than the minimum wage.

### 句子拆分

主干：**U.S. customers historically tipped people**
- `they assumed were earning most of their income via tips`：省略that的定语从句，修饰people
  - `they assumed`：插入语
- `such as restaurant servers earning less than the minimum wage`：插入语，举例
  - `earning less than the minimum wage`：现在分词作后置定语，修饰servers

### 生词

historically：历史上；tip：给小费；assume：认为；earn most of their income via tips：通过小费获得大部分收入；such as：例如；restaurant servers：餐厅服务员；earn less than the minimum wage：收入低于最低工资

### 翻译

美国消费者历史上给那些他们认为通过小费获得大部分收入的人小费，比如收入低于最低工资的餐厅服务员。

### 考点

**省略that的定语从句+插入语+现在分词后置定语**；21题B选项的依据——美国的小费做法被认为对服务员至关重要（was considered essential for waiters = tipped people they assumed were earning most of their income via tips, such as restaurant servers earning less than minimum wage）。
