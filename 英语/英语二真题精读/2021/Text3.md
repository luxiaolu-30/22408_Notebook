

When Microsoft bought task management app 任务管理应用 Wunderlist and mobile calendar 移动日历 Sunrise in 2015, it picked up 获得 two newcomers 新公司 that were attracting considerable buzz 相当大的关注 in Silicon Valley 硅谷. Microsoft's own Office dominates 主导 the market for "productivity" software 生产力软件, but the start-ups 初创公司 represented a new wave of technology designed from the ground up 从头设计 for the smartphone world.

Both apps, however, were later scrapped 废弃, after Microsoft said it had used their best features in its own products. Their teams of engineers stayed on 留下, making them two of the many "acqui-hires 人才收购" that the biggest companies have used to feed their insatiable hunger 无法满足的渴望 for tech talent 科技人才.

To Microsoft's critics 批评者, the fates of Wunderlist and Sunrise are examples of a remorseless drive 无情的驱动力 by Big Tech to chew up 吞噬 any innovative companies that lie in their path. "They bought the seedlings 幼苗 and closed them down," complained Paul Arnold, a partner at San Francisco-based Switch Ventures, putting paid to 终结 businesses that might one day turn into competitors. Microsoft declined to comment 拒绝置评.

Like other start-up investors, Mr Arnold's own business often depends on 依赖 selling start-ups to larger tech companies, though he admits to mixed feelings 复杂感受 about the result: "I think these things are good for me, if I put my selfish hat on 从自私角度. But are they good for the American economy? I don't know."

The US Federal Trade Commission 美国联邦贸易委员会 says it wants to find the answer to that question. This week, it asked the five most valuable US tech companies for information about their many small acquisitions 小型收购 over the past decade. Although only a research project at this stage, the request has raised the prospect 前景 of regulators wading into 介入 early-stage tech markets that until now have been beyond their reach 超出其范围.

Given their combined market value of more than $5.5tn, rifling through 仔细检查 such small deals—many of them much less prominent 不那么显眼 than Wunderlist and Sunrise—might seem beside the point 离题. Between them, the five companies (Apple, Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Facebook) have spent an average of only $3.4bn a year on sub-$1bn acquisitions 十亿美元以下收购 over the past five years—a drop in the ocean 沧海一粟 compared with their massive financial reserves 巨额财务储备, and the more than $130bn of venture capital 风险投资 that was invested in the US last year.

However, critics say that the big companies use such deals to buy their most threatening potential competitors before their businesses have a chance to gain momentum 获得势头, in some cases as part of a "buy and kill" tactic 策略 to simply close them down.

## 长难句1

> When Microsoft bought task management app Wunderlist and mobile calendar Sunrise in 2015, it picked up two newcomers that were attracting considerable buzz in Silicon Valley.

### 句子拆分

主干：**it picked up two newcomers**
- `When Microsoft bought task management app Wunderlist and mobile calendar Sunrise in 2015`：when时间状语从句
- `that were attracting considerable buzz in Silicon Valley`：that定语从句，修饰newcomers

### 生词

Microsoft：微软；task management app：任务管理应用；mobile calendar：移动日历；pick up：获得、收购；newcomer：新公司、新来者；attract：吸引；considerable：相当大的；buzz：关注、热议；Silicon Valley：硅谷

### 翻译

### 考点

# 2021 英二 Text3 31–35｜题目+选项+逐选项解析

> 原文主题：微软2015年收购Wunderlist和Sunrise，后废弃应用但保留工程师团队（人才收购）；批评者认为这是大科技公司吞噬创新公司、消除潜在竞争对手；投资者阿诺德承认对自己有利但担心对美国经济影响；美国联邦贸易委员会要求五大科技公司提供过去十年小型收购信息，可能介入早期市场；五大公司小型收购花费相对巨额储备只是沧海一粟；批评者称大公司用"收购并杀死"策略消除潜在竞争对手

## 31. What is true about Wunderlist and Sunrise after their acquisitions?

[A] Their market values declined. [B] Their tech features improved. [C] Their engineers were retained. [D] Their products were re-priced.

✅答案：**C** 定位：第二段。`Their teams of engineers stayed on, making them two of the many "acqui-hires"`（他们的工程师团队留下了，使他们成为众多"人才收购"中的两个）。说明收购后工程师被保留。

- A：错误。Their market values declined（市值下降）无中生有。
- B：错误。Their tech features improved（技术特性改进）偷换概念。微软说used their best features in its own products（在自己产品中使用了它们的最佳特性），不是原应用技术特性改进。
- C：正确。他们的工程师被保留。teams of engineers stayed on = their engineers were retained。
- D：错误。Their products were re-priced（产品重新定价）无中生有。

## 32. Microsoft's critics believe that the big tech companies tend to ______.

[A] ignore public opinions [B] treat new tech talent unfairly [C] exaggerate their product quality [D] eliminate their potential competitors

✅答案：**D** 定位：第三段。`the fates of Wunderlist and Sunrise are examples of a remorseless drive by Big Tech to chew up any innovative companies that lie in their path`（Wunderlist和Sunrise的命运是大科技公司无情吞噬任何挡路创新公司的例子）+ `putting paid to businesses that might one day turn into competitors`（终结了可能有一天变成竞争对手的企业）。说明批评者认为大科技公司倾向于消除潜在竞争对手。

- A：错误。ignore public opinions（忽视公众意见）无中生有。
- B：错误。treat new tech talent unfairly（不公平对待新科技人才）与原文相反。工程师团队被保留，是人才收购，不是不公平对待。
- C：错误。exaggerate their product quality（夸大产品质量）无中生有。
- D：正确。消除他们的潜在竞争对手。chew up any innovative companies that lie in their path + putting paid to businesses that might turn into competitors = eliminate their potential competitors。

## 33. Paul Arnold is concerned that small acquisitions might ______.

[A] harm the national economy [B] worsen market competition [C] discourage start-up investors [D] weaken big tech companies

✅答案：**A** 定位：第四段。`"I think these things are good for me, if I put my selfish hat on. But are they good for the American economy? I don't know."`（"我认为这些事对我有好处，如果我从自私角度看。但它们对美国经济有好处吗？我不知道。"）说明阿诺德担心小型收购可能损害国家经济。

- A：正确。损害国家经济。are they good for the American economy? I don't know（暗示可能不好）= harm the national economy。
- B：错误。worsen market competition（恶化市场竞争）无中生有。
- C：错误。discourage start-up investors（使初创公司投资者气馁）与原文相反。阿诺德自己的业务依赖卖初创公司，对他有好处。
- D：错误。weaken big tech companies（削弱大科技公司）与原文相反。收购帮助大公司获得人才和消除竞争对手。

## 34. The US Federal Trade Commission intends to ______.

[A] examine small acquisitions [B] limit Big Tech's expansion [C] supervise start-ups' operations [D] encourage research collaboration

✅答案：**A** 定位：第五段。`it asked the five most valuable US tech companies for information about their many small acquisitions over the past decade`（它要求美国五家最有价值的科技公司提供过去十年许多小型收购的信息）+ `raised the prospect of regulators wading into early-stage tech markets`（提高了监管机构介入早期科技市场的前景）。说明联邦贸易委员会打算审查小型收购。

- A：正确。审查小型收购。asked for information about small acquisitions + raised prospect of regulators wading into = examine small acquisitions。
- B：错误。limit Big Tech's expansion（限制大科技公司扩张）偷换概念。只是研究和可能介入，不是限制扩张。
- C：错误。supervise start-ups' operations（监管初创公司运营）无中生有。是监管大公司的收购，不是初创公司运营。
- D：错误。encourage research collaboration（鼓励研究合作）无中生有。

## 35. For the five biggest tech companies, their small acquisitions have ______.

[A] brought little financial pressure [B] raised few management challenges [C] set an example for future deals [D] generated considerable profits

✅答案：**A** 定位：第六段。`have spent an average of only $3.4bn a year on sub-$1bn acquisitions over the past five years—a drop in the ocean compared with their massive financial reserves`（过去五年平均每年仅在十亿美元以下收购上花费34亿美元——与它们的巨额财务储备相比，这是沧海一粟）。说明小型收购几乎没有带来财务压力。

- A：正确。几乎没有带来财务压力。only $3.4bn a year + a drop in the ocean compared with massive financial reserves = brought little financial pressure。
- B：错误。raised few management challenges（几乎没有带来管理挑战）无中生有。
- C：错误。set an example for future deals（为未来交易树立榜样）无中生有。
