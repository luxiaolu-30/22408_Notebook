

We're fairly good at judging people based on first impressions 第一印象, thin slices of experience 经验薄片 ranging from a glimpse of a photo to a five-minute interaction, and deliberation 深思熟虑 can be not only extraneous 无关的 but intrusive 打扰的. In one study of the ability she dubbed 称为 "thin slicing," the late psychologist 已故心理学家 Nalini Ambady asked participants to watch silent 10-second video clips 视频片段 of professors and to rate the instructor's overall effectiveness 整体有效性. Their ratings correlated strongly with 与…强相关 students' end-of-semester ratings. Another set of participants had to count backward 倒数 from 1,000 by nines as they watched the clips, occupying their conscious working memory 有意识工作记忆. Their ratings were just as accurate, demonstrating the intuitive nature 直觉本质 of the social processing 社会处理.

Critically 关键地, another group was asked to spend a minute writing down reasons for their judgment, before giving the rating. Accuracy dropped dramatically 急剧下降. Ambady suspected that deliberation focused them on vivid but misleading cues 生动但误导的线索, such as certain gestures or utterances 话语, rather than letting the complex interplay 复杂相互作用 of subtle signals 微妙信号 form a holistic impression 整体印象. She found similar interference 干扰 when participants watched 15-second clips of pairs of people and judged whether they were strangers, friends, or dating partners 约会对象.

Other research shows we're better at detecting deception 检测欺骗 and sexual orientation 性取向 from thin slices when we rely on intuition 直觉 instead of reflection 反思. "It's as if you're driving a stick shift 手动挡," says Judith Hall, a psychologist at Northeastern University, "and if you start thinking about it too much, you can't remember what you're doing. But if you go on automatic pilot 自动驾驶, you're fine. Much of our social life is like that."

Thinking too much can also harm our ability to form preferences 形成偏好. College students' ratings of strawberry jams 草莓酱 and college courses aligned better with 与…更一致 experts' opinions when the students weren't asked to analyze their rationale 分析理由. And people made car-buying decisions that were both objectively better 客观上更好 and more personally satisfying 更个人满意 when asked to focus on their feelings rather than on details, but only if the decision was complex—when they had a lot of information to process.

Intuition's special powers are unleashed 释放 only in certain circumstances. In one study, participants completed a battery of 一组 eight tasks, including four that tapped reflective thinking 反思思维 (discerning rules 辨别规则, comprehending vocabulary 理解词汇) and four that tapped intuition and creativity (generating new products or figures of speech 修辞). Then they rated the degree to which they had used intuition ("gut feelings 直觉," "hunches 预感," "my heart"). Use of their gut hurt their performance on the first four tasks, as expected, and helped them on the rest. Sometimes the heart is smarter than the head.
