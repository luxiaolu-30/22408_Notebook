

Today, widespread social pressure 普遍的社会压力 to immediately go to college in conjunction with 与…一起 increasingly high expectations in a fast-moving world often causes students to completely overlook 忽视 the possibility of taking a gap year 间隔年. After all, if everyone you know is going to college in the fall, it seems silly to stay back 推迟一年 a year, doesn't it? And after going to school for 12 years, it doesn't feel natural to spend a year doing something that isn't academic 学术的.

But while this may be true, it's not a good enough reason to condemn 谴责 gap years. There's always a constant fear of falling behind everyone else on the socially perpetuated 社会延续的 "race to the finish line," whether that be toward graduate school, medical school or a lucrative 赚钱的 career. But despite common misconceptions 误解, a gap year does not impede 阻碍 the success of academic pursuits 学术追求—in fact, it probably enhances 促进 it.

Studies from the United States and Australia show that students who take a gap year are generally better prepared for and perform better in college than those who do not. Rather than pulling students back, a gap year pushes them ahead by preparing them for independence 独立, new responsibilities and environmental changes—all things that first-year students often struggle with the most. Gap year experiences can lessen the blow 减轻打击 when it comes to adjusting to college and being thrown into a brand new environment, making it easier to focus on academics and activities rather than acclimation 适应 blunders 失误.

If you're not convinced of the inherent value 内在价值 in taking a year off to explore interests, then consider its financial impact on future academic choices. According to the National Center for Education Statistics, nearly 80 percent of college students end up changing their majors 专业 at least once. This isn't surprising, considering the basic mandatory 必修的 high school curriculum leaves students with a poor understanding of the vast academic possibilities that await them in college. Many students find themselves listing one major on their college applications, but switching to another after taking college classes. It's not necessarily a bad thing, but depending on the school, it can be costly to make up credits 补学分 after switching too late in the game. At Boston College, for example, you would have to complete an extra year were you to switch to the nursing school from another department. Taking a gap year to figure things out initially 最初 can help prevent stress and save money later on.

## 31. One of the reasons for high-school graduates not taking a gap year is that ______.

[A] they think it academically misleading [B] they have a lot of fun to explore in college [C] it feels strange to do differently from others [D] it seems worthless to take off-campus courses

✅答案：**C** 定位：第一段。`if everyone you know is going to college in the fall, it seems silly to stay back a year`（如果你认识的每个人都在秋天上大学，推迟一年看起来很傻）。和别人不一样（推迟一年）感觉很奇怪。

- A：错误。they think it academically misleading（他们认为这在学术上有误导性）无中生有。原文说spend a year doing something that isn't academic（花一年做非学术的事感觉不自然），不是误导。
- B：错误。they have a lot of fun to explore in college（他们在大学有很多乐趣可以探索）无中生有。
- C：正确。和别人做不一样的事感觉奇怪。everyone is going to college, stay back a year seems silly = it feels strange to do differently from others。
- D：错误。it seems worthless to take off-campus courses（修校外课程似乎毫无价值）无中生有。

## 32. Studies from the US and Australia imply that taking a gap year helps ______.

[A] keep students from being unrealistic [B] lower risks in choosing careers [C] ease freshmen's financial burdens [D] relieve freshmen of pressures

✅答案：**D** 定位：第三段。`students who take a gap year are generally better prepared for and perform better in college`（休间隔年的学生通常准备得更充分、表现更好）+ `preparing them for independence, new responsibilities and environmental changes—all things that first-year students often struggle with the most`（让他们为独立、新责任和环境变化做好准备——这些都是一年级学生最挣扎的）+ `lessen the blow when it comes to adjusting to college`（减轻适应大学的打击）。说明间隔年帮助新生减轻压力。

- A：错误。keep students from being unrealistic（防止学生不切实际）无中生有。
- B：错误。lower risks in choosing careers（降低职业选择风险）无中生有。文章讲的是学术选择/专业，不是职业。
- C：错误。ease freshmen's financial burdens（减轻新生经济负担）偷换概念。经济负担是第四段讲的换专业省钱，不是美国和澳大利亚研究的内容。
- D：正确。减轻新生压力。better prepared + lessen the blow of adjusting to college = relieve freshmen of pressures。

## 34. A gap year may save money for students by helping them ______.

[A] avoid academic failures [B] establish long-term goals [C] switch to another college [D] decide on the right major

✅答案：**D** 定位：第四段。`nearly 80 percent of college students end up changing their majors at least once`（近80%的大学生最终至少换一次专业）+ `it can be costly to make up credits after switching too late`（换专业太晚补学分可能很费钱）+ `Taking a gap year to figure things out initially can help prevent stress and save money later on`（间隔年最初弄清楚可以帮助预防压力和以后省钱）。说明间隔年通过帮助学生决定正确专业来省钱。

- A：错误。avoid academic failures（避免学术失败）无中生有。
- B：错误。establish long-term goals（建立长期目标）无中生有。
- C：错误。switch to another college（转到另一所大学）偷换概念。原文是switch majors（换专业），不是switch college（转大学）。
- D：正确。决定正确的专业。figure things out initially + avoid switching majors too late = decide on the right major。

## 35. The most suitable title for this text would be ______.

[A] In Favor of the Gap Year [B] The ABCs of the Gap Year [C] The Gap Year Comes Back [D] The Gap Year: A Dilemma

✅答案：**A** 主旨题。全文主线：学生忽视间隔年→但间隔年不阻碍学术成功，反而促进→研究表明间隔年让学生更好准备、减轻压力→间隔年还能帮助确定专业、省钱。全文支持间隔年。

- A：正确。支持间隔年。全文论点是间隔年有益（enhances academic success, relieves pressures, saves money），方向是支持。
- B：错误。间隔年的基础知识。文章不是介绍间隔年是什么，而是论证间隔年的好处。
- C：错误。间隔年回归。文章没有说间隔年曾经消失现在回归。
- D：错误。间隔年：一个困境。文章明确支持间隔年，不是说困境。

### 本篇命题规律小结（英二阅读）

1. 教育类文章：关注**现象（忽视间隔年）→反驳（间隔年有益）→证据1（研究：减轻压力）→证据2（经济：省钱）→结论**的结构
2. 原因题：定位到**if条件句**（if everyone is going to college, it seems silly to stay back）
3. 推理题：定位到**研究结果+具体解释**（better prepared + lessen the blow = relieve pressures）
4. 词义题：结合**上下文同义词**（adjusting to college = acclimation = adaptation）
5. 主旨题：把握**全文方向**（支持间隔年），不选基础知识（ABCs）、回归（comes back）或困境（dilemma）
