In her new book Cogs and Monsters: What Economics Is, and What It Should Be, Diane Coyle, an economist at Cambridge University, argues that the digital economy requires new ways of thinking about progress. "Whatever we mean by the economy growing, by things getting better, the gains will have to be more evenly shared 更均衡地共享 than in the recent past," she writes. "An economy of tech millionaires or billionaires and gig workers 零工工人, with middle-income jobs undercut by 被…削弱 automation, will not be politically sustainable 政治上可持续的."

Improving living standards and increasing prosperity 繁荣 for more people will require greater use of digital technologies to boost productivity 提高生产率 in various sectors, including health care and construction, says Coyle. But people can't be expected to embrace 接受 the changes if they're not seeing the benefits—if they're just seeing good jobs being destroyed.

In a recent interview, Coyle said she fears that tech's inequality problem could be a roadblock 障碍 to deploying AI. "We're talking about disruption 颠覆," she says. "These are transformative technologies 变革性技术 that change the ways we spend our time every day, that change business models that succeed." To make such "tremendous changes," she adds, you need social buy-in 社会认同.

Instead, says Coyle, resentment 不满 is simmering 酝酿 among many as the benefits are perceived to go to elites 精英 in a handful of prosperous cities.

According to the Brookings Institution, a short list of eight American cities that included San Francisco, San Jose, Boston, and Seattle had roughly 38% of all tech jobs by 2019. New AI technologies are particularly concentrated: Brookings's Mark Muro and Sifan Liu estimate that just 15 cities account for two-thirds of the AI assets and capabilities in the United States.

The dominance of a few cities in the invention and commercialization of AI means that geographical disparities 地理差距 in wealth will continue to soar 飙升. Not only will this foster political and social unrest 动荡, but it could, as Coyle suggests, hold back 阻碍 the sorts of AI technologies needed for regional economies to grow.

Part of the solution could lie in somehow loosening 放松 the stranglehold 束缚 that Big Tech has on defining the AI agenda. That will likely take increased federal funding for research independent of the tech giants.

A more immediate response is to broaden our digital imaginations to conceive of 构想 AI technologies that don't simply replace jobs but expand opportunities in the sectors that different parts of the country care most about, like health care, education, and manufacturing.

# 2024英二 Text1 长难句精读（原文+拆分+语法+翻译+考点）

## 长难句1

> "Whatever we mean by the economy growing, by things getting better, the gains will have to be more evenly shared than in the recent past," she writes.

### 句子拆分

主干：**the gains will have to be more evenly shared than in the recent past**
- `Whatever we mean by the economy growing, by things getting better`：whatever让步状语从句
- `she writes`：倒装引语

### 生词

whatever：无论什么；mean by：意思是；economy growing：经济增长；things getting better：事物变好；gains：收益；have to：必须；more evenly shared：更均衡地共享；than in the recent past：比最近的过去

### 翻译

"无论我们认为经济增长意味着什么，或事物的改进意味着什么，收益将必须比最近的过去更均衡地由大家共享，"她写道。

### 考点

**whatever让步状语从句+比较级+倒装引语**；21题C选项的依据——Coyle认为经济增长应该让人们平等受益（benefit people equally = gains more evenly shared）。

### 考点

**两个动名词并列作主语+不定式目的状语+插入语举例+倒装引语**；22题C选项的依据——数字技术应该被用来提高整体工作效率（raise overall work efficiency = boost productivity in various sectors）。

### 考点

**被动语态+if条件状语从句+破折号解释**；23题D选项的背景——如果人们看不到好处只看到工作被摧毁，就不会接受变化，说明变革性技术可能不受公众欢迎。
