Reskilling 再培训 is something that sounds like a buzzword 流行词 but is actually a requirement if we plan to have a future where a lot of would-be workers 准工人 do not get left behind 落后.

We know we are moving into a period where the jobs in demand 需求中的工作 will change rapidly, as will the requirements of the jobs that remain. Research by the WEF 世界经济论坛 detailed in the Harvard Business Review, finds that on average 42 per cent of the core skills "within job roles will change by 2022. That is a very short timeline, so we can only imagine what the changes will be further in the future.

The question of who should pay for reskilling is a thorny 棘手的 one. For individual companies, the temptation 诱惑 is always to let go of 解雇 workers whose skills are no longer in demand and replace them with those whose skills are. That does not always happen. AT&T is often given as the gold standard 黄金标准 of a company who decided to do a massive reskilling program rather than go with a fire-and-hire strategy 解雇再雇佣策略, ultimately retraining 18,000 employees. Prepandemic 疫情前, other companies including Amazon and Disney had also pledged 承诺 to create their own plans. When the skills mismatch 技能错配 is in the broader economy though, the focus usually turns to government to handle. Efforts in Canada and elsewhere have been arguably languid 懒散的 at best, and have given us a situation where we frequently hear of employers begging for workers even at times and in regions where unemployment is high.

With the pandemic, unemployment is very high indeed. In February, at 3.5 per cent and 5.5 per cent respectively 分别地, unemployment rates in Canada and the United States were at generational lows 几代人低点 and worker shortages were everywhere. As of May, those rates had spiked 飙升 up to 13.3 per cent and 13.7 per cent, and although many worker shortages had disappeared, not all had done so. In the medical field, to take an obvious example, the pandemic meant that there were still clear shortages of doctors, nurses and other medical personnel 医务人员.

Of course, it is not like you can take an unemployed waiter and train him to be a doctor in a few weeks, no matter who pays for it. But even if you cannot close that gap, maybe you can close others, and doing so would be to the benefit of all concerned 所有相关方. That seems to be the case in Sweden, where the pandemic kick-started 启动 a retraining program where business as well as government had a role.

Reskilling in this way would be challenging in a North American context 北美背景. You can easily imagine a chorus of 齐声 "you can't do that," because teachers or nurses or whoever have special skills, and using any support staff who has been quickly trained is bound to 一定会 end in disaster. Maybe. Or maybe it is something that can work well in Sweden, with its history of co-operation between business, labour and government, but not in North America where our history is very different. Then again, maybe it is akin to 类似于 wartime, when extraordinary things take place, but it is business as usual 一切照旧 after the fact. And yet, as in war the pandemic is teaching us that many things, including rapid reskilling, can be done if there is a will to do them. In any case, Sweden's workforce is now more skilled, in more things, and more flexible than it was before.

Of course, reskilling programs, whether for pandemic needs or the post-pandemic world, are expensive and at a time when everyone's budgets are lean 拮据 this may not be the time to implement them. Then again, extending income support programs to get us through the next months is expensive, too, to say nothing of 更不用说 the cost of having a swath of 一大批 long-term unemployed in the post-COVID years. Given that, perhaps we should think hard about whether the pandemic can jump-start us to a place where reskilling becomes much more than a buzzword.

## 长难句1

> Reskilling is something that sounds like a buzzword but is actually a requirement if we plan to have a future where a lot of would-be workers do not get left behind.

### 句子拆分

主干：**Reskilling is something**
- `that sounds like a buzzword but is actually a requirement`：that定语从句，修饰something，but连接两个并列谓语
- `if we plan to have a future where a lot of would-be workers do not get left behind`：if条件状语从句
  - `where a lot of would-be workers do not get left behind`：where定语从句，修饰future

### 生词

reskilling：再培训；buzzword：流行词、时髦术语；requirement：要求、必要条件；plan to：计划；would-be workers：准工人、未来工人；get left behind：被落下、落后

### 翻译

再培训听起来像是一个流行词，但实际上如果我们计划拥有一个许多准工人不会被落下的未来，它就是一个必要条件。

### 考点

**that定语从句+but并列谓语+if条件状语从句+where定语从句**；开篇点题——再培训是必要的，不是流行词。

### 考点

# 2021 英二 Text1 21–25｜题目+选项+逐选项解析

> 原文主题：再培训不是流行词而是必要条件——工作和技能要求快速变化，42%核心技能将改变；谁来付费是棘手问题，AT&T选择大规模再培训而非解雇再雇佣；加拿大等政府努力不足；疫情后失业率飙升但医疗领域仍缺人；瑞典启动企业+政府再培训项目，北欧航空培训下岗员工支持医院；结论：疫情教会我们快速再培训可以做到

## 21. Research by the World Economic Forum suggests ______.

[A] an increase in full-time employment [B] an urgent demand for new job skills [C] a steady growth of job opportunities [D] a controversy about the "core skills"

✅答案：**B** 定位：第二段。`on average 42 per cent of the core skills within job roles will change by 2022`（到2022年，工作角色中平均42%的核心技能将发生变化）。说明对新工作技能有迫切需求。

- A：错误。an increase in full-time employment（全职就业增加）无中生有。
- B：正确。对新工作技能的迫切需求。42% core skills will change = urgent demand for new job skills。
- C：错误。a steady growth of job opportunities（工作机会稳步增长）无中生有。
- D：错误。a controversy about the "core skills"（关于"核心技能"的争议）无中生有。

## 22. AT&T is cited to show ______.

[A] an alternative to the fire-and-hire strategy [B] an immediate need for government support [C] the importance of staff appraisal standards [D] the characteristics of reskilling programs

✅答案：**A** 定位：第三段。`AT&T is often given as the gold standard of a company who decided to do a massive reskilling program rather than go with a fire-and-hire strategy`（AT&T经常被视为一家公司的黄金标准，它决定做一个大规模的再培训项目，而不是采用解雇再雇佣策略）。说明AT&T是解雇再雇佣策略的替代方案。

- A：正确。解雇再雇佣策略的替代方案。do a massive reskilling program rather than go with a fire-and-hire strategy = an alternative to the fire-and-hire strategy。
- B：错误。an immediate need for government support（迫切需要政府支持）无中生有。
- C：错误。the importance of staff appraisal standards（员工评估标准的重要性）无中生有。
- D：错误。the characteristics of reskilling programs（再培训项目的特征）偷换概念。AT&T是再培训的例子，不是展示再培训项目的特征。

## 23. Efforts to resolve the skills mismatch in Canada ______.

[A] have driven up labour costs [B] have proved to be inconsistent [C] have met with fierce opposition [D] have appeared to be insufficient

✅答案：**D** 定位：第三段。`Efforts in Canada and elsewhere have been arguably languid at best, and have given us a situation where we frequently hear of employers begging for workers even at times and in regions where unemployment is high`（加拿大和其他地方的努力充其量可以说是无力的，给了我们一个情况：我们频繁听说雇主乞求工人，即使在失业率高的时期和地区）。说明加拿大的努力似乎不足。

- A：错误。have driven up labour costs（推高了劳动力成本）无中生有。
- B：错误。have proved to be inconsistent（被证明不一致）无中生有。
- C：错误。have met with fierce opposition（遭到激烈反对）无中生有。
- D：正确。似乎不足。languid at best + employers begging for workers despite high unemployment = have appeared to be insufficient。

## 24. We can learn from Paragraph 3 that there was ______.

[A] a call for policy adjustment [B] a change in hiring practices [C] a lack of medical workers [D] a sign of economic recovery

✅答案：**C** 定位：第四段（注意原文段落编号，题目说Paragraph 3实际指疫情失业那段）。`In the medical field, to take an obvious example, the pandemic meant that there were still clear shortages of doctors, nurses and other medical personnel`（在医疗领域，举一个明显的例子，大流行病意味着医生、护士和其他医务人员仍然明显短缺）。说明缺乏医务人员。

- B：错误。a change in hiring practices（招聘做法的变化）无中生有。
- C：正确。缺乏医务人员。clear shortages of doctors, nurses and other medical personnel = a lack of medical workers。
- D：错误。a sign of economic recovery（经济复苏的迹象）与原文相反。失业率飙升，不是复苏。

## 25. Scandinavian Airlines decided to ______.

[A] create job vacancies for the unemployed [B] prepare their laid-off workers for other jobs [C] retrain their cabin staff for better services [D] finance their staff's college education

✅答案：**B** 定位：第五段（瑞典案例）。`When forced to furlough 90 per cent of their cabin staff, Scandinavian Airlines decided to start up a short retraining program that reskilled the laid-off workers to support hospital staff`（当被迫让90%的客舱员工休假时，北欧航空决定启动一个短期再培训项目，对下岗员工进行再培训以支持医院工作人员）。说明北欧航空准备让下岗员工做其他工作。

- A：错误。create job vacancies for the unemployed（为失业者创造职位空缺）偷换概念。是再培训下岗员工，不是创造职位空缺。
- B：正确。让下岗员工为其他工作做准备。reskilled the laid-off workers to support hospital staff = prepare their laid-off workers for other jobs。
- C：错误。retrain their cabin staff for better services（再培训客舱员工以提供更好服务）偷换概念。是培训去支持医院工作人员，不是更好的客舱服务。
- D：错误。finance their staff's college education（资助员工的大学教育）无中生有。

### 本篇命题规律小结（英二阅读）

1. 社会/经济类文章：关注**概念引入（再培训是必要条件）→背景（技能快速变化）→问题（谁付费）→企业案例（AT&T再培训）→政府努力不足（加拿大）→疫情冲击（失业但医疗缺人）→解决方案（瑞典再培训项目）→结论（疫情教会我们快速再培训可行）**的结构
