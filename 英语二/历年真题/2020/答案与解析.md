#### 重点解析

- **第1题（D tricky）**：词义辨析+语境。首句为主题句"每位父母都想成为好父母"，but表转折，"定义好父母无疑很棘手"，since给出原因"不同孩子对同样养育方式反应不同"。tricky=棘手的，其余tedious乏味的、pleasant愉快的、instructive有教育意义的均不符。

- **第2题（B for example）**：逻辑关系。前句说"不同孩子反应不同"，本句举例"冷静守规则的孩子可能比弟弟妹妹反应更好"，用for example举例，其余in addition另外、at once立刻、by accident偶然均不符。

- **第3题（A fortunately）**：副词辨析+语境。第一段说定义好父母很难，这里转折"幸好还有一类父母更容易描述"，fortunately=幸运地，符合。

- **第5题（A while）**：逻辑连词。"虽然每位父母都想耐心，但这不容易"，逗号前后为让步转折关系，while=虽然，符合。

- **第8题（C push）**：动词辨析+语境。"你只是凡人，有时孩子会把你逼得太紧"，push=催促/紧逼，move移动、drag拖拽、send发送均不符。

- **第10题（C harsh）**：词义辨析+语境。"失去耐心对孩子大吼，或说些太刻薄的话"，harsh=刺耳的/刻薄的，为贬义，boring乏味、naive天真、vague模糊均不符。

- **第12题（C however）**：逻辑连词。上段描述父母失去耐心（普遍现象），本段转折"然而，即使很普遍，也要牢记……"，however表转折。

- **第15题（D cool）**：固定搭配。"lose one's cool"=失去冷静/发脾气，与全文主题（耐心、情绪控制）呼应。time/bond/race均不搭配。

- **第17题（D confronted）**：动宾搭配。"面对压力时"=confronted by stress，cheated欺骗、defeated打败、confused困惑均不符。

- **第20题（B emerge）**：动宾搭配+语境。"你和孩子们将受益，从压力时刻中走出来，身心感觉更好"，emerge=浮现/出来，hide隐藏、withdraw撤退、escape逃避均含负面义不符。

Rats and other animals need to be highly attuned to 适应 social signals from others so they can identify friends to cooperate with and enemies to avoid. To find out if this extends to 延伸到 non-living beings, Laleh Quinn at the University of California, San Diego, and her colleagues tested whether rats can detect social signals from robotic rats 机器老鼠.

They housed 饲养 eight adult rats with two types of robotic rat—one social and one asocial 非社交的—for four days. The robot rats were quite minimalist 极简主义的, resembling a chunkier 更粗壮的 version of a computer mouse with wheels to move around and colourful markings 彩色标记.

During the experiment, the social robot rat followed the living rats around, played with the same toys, and opened cage doors to let trapped rats escape. Meanwhile, the asocial robot simply moved forwards and backwards and side to side 左右.

Next, the researchers trapped the robots in cages and gave the rats the opportunity to release them by pressing a lever 杠杆. Across 18 trials each, the living rats were 52 percent more likely on average to set the social robot free than the asocial one. This suggests that the rats perceived the social robot as a genuine social being 真正的社会存在. They may have bonded more with the social robot because it displayed behaviours like communal exploring 共同探索 and playing. This could lead to the rats better remembering having freed it earlier, and wanting the robot to return the favour 回报 when they get trapped, says Quinn.

The readiness 意愿 of the rats to befriend the social robot was surprising given its minimal design. The robot was the same size as a regular rat but resembled a simple plastic box on wheels. "We'd assumed we'd have to give it a moving head and tail, facial features, and put a scent 气味 on it to make it smell like a real rat, but that wasn't necessary," says Janet Wiles at the University of Queensland in Australia, who helped with the research.

The finding shows how sensitive rats are to social cues 社会线索, even when they come from basic robots. Similarly, children tend to treat robots as if they are fellow beings 同类, even when they display only simple social signals. "We humans seem to be fascinated by 着迷于 robots, and it turns out other animals are too," says Wiles.

#### 长难句1

> Rats and other animals need to be highly attuned to social signals from others so they can identify friends to cooperate with and enemies to avoid.

##### 句子拆分

主干：**Rats and other animals need to be highly attuned to social signals from others**
- `so they can identify friends to cooperate with and enemies to avoid`：so目的状语从句
  - `to cooperate with`和`to avoid`是不定式作后置定语，分别修饰friends和enemies

##### 生词

attuned to：适应、熟悉；social signals：社会信号；identify：识别；cooperate with：与…合作；avoid：避免

##### 翻译

##### 考点

**so目的状语从句+不定式后置定语**；开篇背景——动物需要社会信号，引出测试老鼠是否能从机器人身上检测社会信号。

#### 21. Quinn and her colleagues conducted a test to see if rats can ______.

[A] distinguish a friendly rat from a hostile one [B] pick up social signals from non-living rats [C] attain sociable traits through special training [D] send out warning messages to their fellow

✅答案：**B** 定位：第一段。`To find out if this extends to non-living beings, … tested whether rats can detect social signals from robotic rats`（为了查明这是否延伸到非生物，测试了老鼠是否能从机器老鼠身上检测社会信号）。说明测试目的是看老鼠是否能从非生物（机器老鼠）身上获取社会信号。

- A：错误。distinguish a friendly rat from a hostile one（区分友好老鼠和敌对老鼠）是真实老鼠的能力，不是测试目的。
- B：正确。从非生物老鼠身上获取社会信号。detect social signals from robotic rats = pick up social signals from non-living rats。
- C：错误。attain sociable traits through special training（通过特殊训练获得社交特征）无中生有。
- D：错误。send out warning messages to their fellow（向同伴发出警告信息）无中生有。

It is true that CEO pay has gone up—top ones may make 300 times the pay of typical workers on average, and since the mid-1970s, CEO pay for large publicly traded 公开交易的 American corporations has, by varying estimates, gone up by about 500%. The typical CEO of a top American corporation now makes about $18.9 million a year.

The best model for understanding the growth of CEO pay is that of limited CEO talent in a world where business opportunities for the top firms are rapidly growing. The efforts of America's highest-earning 1% have been one of the more dynamic elements of the global economy. It's not popular to say, but one reason their pay has gone up so much is that CEOs really have upped their game 提升了表现 relative to many other workers in the U.S. economy.

Today's CEO, at least for major American firms, must have many more skills than simply being able to "run the company." CEOs must have a good sense of financial markets 金融市场 and maybe even how the company should trade in them. They also need better public relations skills 公共关系技能 than their predecessors 前任, as the costs of even a minor slipup 小失误 can be significant. Then there's the fact that large American companies are much more globalized 全球化 than ever before, with supply chains 供应链 spread across a larger number of countries. To lead in that system requires knowledge that is fairly mind-boggling 令人难以置信的. Plus, virtually all major American companies are becoming tech companies, often with their own research and development 研发. And beyond this, major CEOs still have to do all the day-to-day work they have always done.

The common idea that high CEO pay is mainly about ripping people off 剥削 people doesn't explain history very well. By most measures, corporate governance 公司治理 has become a lot tighter and more rigorous 更严格 since the 1970s. Yet it is principally during this period of stronger governance that CEO pay has been high and rising. That suggests it is in the broader corporate interest 公司整体利益 to recruit top candidates for increasingly tough jobs.

Furthermore, the highest CEO salaries are paid to outside candidates 外部候选人, not to the cozy insider picks 内部人选, another sign that high CEO pay is not some kind of depredation 掠夺 at the expense of the rest of the company. And the stock market reacts positively when companies tie CEO pay to, say, stock prices, a sign that those practices build up corporate value 公司价值 not just for the CEO.

✅答案：**A** 定位：最后一段。`Millennials wanted more flexibility in their lives`（千禧一代想要更多生活灵活性）+ `Generation Zs are looking for more certainty and stability … they are quite risk averse`（Z世代正在寻找更多的确定性和稳定性……他们相当规避风险）。说明与千禧一代相比，Z世代更不冒险。

- A：正确。不那么爱冒险。looking for certainty and stability + risk averse = less adventurous。
- B：错误。less realistic（不那么务实）与原文相反。Z世代是economic pragmatists（经济实用主义者），更务实。
- C：错误。more generous（更慷慨）无中生有。
- D：错误。more diligent（更勤奋）无中生有。

---

##### 本篇命题规律小结（英二阅读）

1. 社会/代际类文章：关注**背景（Z世代毕业，关注度上升）→特征（务实谨慎）→原因（2008年危机经历）→数据（88%选专业想着工作，最看重稳定就业）→对比（千禧一代追求灵活性 vs Z世代追求确定性稳定性）**的结构
2. 细节题：定位到**now that原因状语+主句**（attention has been rising steadily）
3. 推理题：定位到**what宾语从句+when非限制性定语从句**（know what an economic train wreck looks like + 2008 crash）
4. 词义题：通过**上下文逻辑**（booming economy done little to ___ anxious urgency → relieve）
5. 对比题：定位到**人物直接引语**（Millennials wanted flexibility vs Zs looking for certainty and stability, risk averse）

##### 新题型 Part B（41-45）

#### 题型说明

**小标题匹配题（Matching Headings）**：从7个备选小标题（A-G）中为5个段落选择最合适的小标题，有2个多余选项。考查段落主旨概括能力。

#### 原文

**Five Ways to Win Over Everyone in the Office**

Is it possible to like everyone in your office? Think about how tough it is to get together 15 people, much less 50, who all get along perfectly. But unlike in friendships, you need coworkers. You work with them every day and you depend on them just as they depend on you. Here are some ways that you can get the whole office on your side.

**41. ______**

If you have a bone to pick with someone in your workplace 与某人有争执, you may try to stay tight-lipped around them. But you won't be helping either one of you. A Harvard Business School study found that observers consistently rated those who were frank about themselves more highly, while those who hid lost trustworthiness 可信度. The lesson is not that you should make your personal life an open book, but rather, when given the option to offer up details about yourself or painstakingly conceal 煞费苦心地隐藏 them, you should just be honest.

**42. ______**

Just as important as being honest about yourself is being receptive to others 善于倾听他人. We often feel the need to tell others how we feel, whether it's a concern about a project, a stray thought, or a compliment. Those are all valid, but you need to take time to hear out your coworkers, too. In fact, rushing to get your own ideas out there can cause colleagues to feel you don't value their opinions. Do your best to engage coworkers in a genuine, back-and-forth conversation, rather than prioritizing your own thoughts.

**43. ______**

It's common to have a "cubicle mate" 隔壁工位的同事 or special confidant 知己 in a work setting. But in addition to those trusted coworkers, you should expand your horizons and find out about all the people around you. Use your lunch and coffee breaks to meet up with colleagues you don't always see. Find out about their lives and interests beyond the job. It requires minimal effort and goes a long way. This will help to grow your internal network, in addition to being a nice break in the work day.

**44. ______**

Positive feedback is important for anyone to hear. And you don't have to be someone's boss to tell them they did an exceptional job on a particular project. This will help engender 产生 good will in others. But don't overdo it or be fake about it. One study found that people responded best to comments that shifted from negative to positive, possibly because it suggested they had won somebody over.

**45. ______**

This one may be a bit more difficult to pull off, but it can go a long way to achieving results. Remember in dealing with any coworker what they appreciate from an interaction. Watch out for how they verbalize 表达 with others. Some people like small talk in a meeting before digging into important matters, while others are more straightforward. Jokes that work for one person won't necessarily land with another. So, adapt your style accordingly to type. Consider the person that you're dealing with in advance and what will get you to your desired outcome.

#### 题目

从以下选项中为段落41-45选择最合适的小标题（A-G），其中两个为多余选项：

[A] Give compliments, just not too many. 适度赞美
[B] Put on a good face, always. 始终保持好面色
[C] Tailor your interactions. 因人而异地交流
[D] Spend time with everyone. 与每个人相处
[E] Reveal, don't hide, information. 透露而非隐藏信息
[F] Slow down and listen. 放慢节奏，倾听他人
[G] Put yourselves in others' shoes. 设身处地为他人着想

---
##### 参考答案
41.E 42.F 43.D 44.A 45.C

#### 解析

**题型特点**：小标题匹配题要求概括段落主旨。解题核心是抓住段落中心句（通常在段首或段尾），理解段落核心论点，再与选项对应。

**解题策略**：
1. 先读选项，标记关键词（动词短语往往是核心）
2. 回到段落，找中心句或转折后的总结句
3. 注意段落高频词与选项词汇的呼应

**逐段解析**：
- **41题 E**：段落讲"不要隐藏信息、要坦诚"，哈佛研究证明坦诚者获更高评价、隐藏者失去信任。核心词frank/honest/conceal对应E的Reveal/hide。
- **42题 F**：段落核心句"rushing to get your own ideas out there can cause colleagues to feel you don't value their opinions"——急着表达自己的观点会让同事觉得你不重视他们，因此要"放慢节奏，倾听"。对应F。
- **43题 D**：段落转折句"you should expand your horizons and find out about all the people around you"，利用午餐和咖啡时间去认识不常见的同事，对应D"与每个人相处"。
- **44题 A**：段落讲正面反馈（positive feedback）很重要，但"don't overdo it or be fake"不要过度或虚假，对应A"适度赞美"。
- **45题 C**：段落最后一句"So, adapt your style accordingly to type"——因人而异地调整风格，对应C"因人而异地交流"。核心词adapt/tailor呼应。
