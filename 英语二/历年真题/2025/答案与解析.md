#### 重点解析

- **第1题（D question）**：词义辨析。"this question"指代前文"为何难以开口求助"这个问题，question=问题。illusion幻觉、discussion讨论、tradition传统均不符。

- **第2题（A reluctance）**：词义辨析。"人们普遍不愿求助导致了高调失败"，reluctance=不愿/勉强。ambition野心、tendency倾向、enthusiasm热情均不符。

- **第3题（C courage）**：词义辨析。"求助需要勇气"，courage=勇气，因为求助意味着承认自己的弱点。attention注意、talent才能、patience耐心均不符。

- **第7题（A Once）**：逻辑连词。"一旦有人开始帮忙，他们可能会接管"，Once=一旦，引导条件状语从句。Unless除非、Although虽然、Before在……之前均不符。

- **第9题（D inconveniencing）**：词义辨析。"成为讨厌的人或给对方添麻烦"，inconveniencing=给……带来不便。overpraising过度赞美、outperforming胜过、reassessing重新评估均不符。

- **第13题（D overcome）**：词义辨析。"要克服这些困难"，overcome=克服。explain解释、identify识别、predict预测均不符。

- **第14题（D fear）**：词义辨析。"你可能害怕给人无能的印象"，fear=害怕/担心。deny否认、forget忘记、miss错过均不符。

- **第15题（B perceived）**：词义辨析。"研究表明寻求建议者被认为更有能力"，perceived as=被认为。disguised伪装、followed跟随、introduced介绍均不符。

- **第17题（C willing）**：词义辨析。"求助者低估了别人有多愿意帮忙"，willing=愿意的。brave勇敢的、disappointed失望的、hesitant犹豫的均不符。

- **第20题（C busy）**：词义辨析。"他们可能只是太忙了"，busy=忙碌的。polite礼貌的、proud骄傲的、lazy懒惰的均不符语境（对方不能帮忙不是因为礼貌或懒，而是太忙）。

U.S. customers historically tipped 给小费 people they assumed were earning most of their income via tips, such as restaurant servers earning less than the minimum wage 最低工资. In the early 2010s, a wide range of businesses started processing purchases with iPads and other digital payment systems 数字支付系统. These systems often prompted 提示 customers to tip for services that were not previously tipped. Today's tip requests are not connected to the salary and service norms 规范 that used to determine when and how people tip. Customers in the past merely always paid tips after receiving service, such as at the conclusion 结束 of a restaurant meal, after getting a haircut or once a pizza was delivered. That timing could reward high-quality service and give workers an incentive 激励 to provide it. It's becoming more common for tips to be requested beforehand 事先. And now tipping technology may even automatically add tips.

The prevalence 普及 of digital payment devices has made it easier to ask customers for a tip. That helps explain why tip requests are creeping into 悄悄进入 new kinds of services. Customers now routinely see menus of suggested default options 默认选项—often well above 20% of what they owe. The amounts have risen from 10% or less in the 1950s to 15% around the year 2000 to 20% or higher today. This increase is sometimes called tipflation 小费通胀—the expectation of ever-higher tip amounts. Tipping has always been a vital source of income for workers in historically tipped services, like restaurants, where the tipped minimum wage can be as low as US $2.13 an hour. Tip creep 小费蔓延 and tipflation are now further supplementing 补充 the income of many low-wage service workers.

Notably, tipping primarily benefits some of these workers, such as servers, but not others, such as cooks and dishwashers 洗碗机工, so to ensure that all employees were paid fair wages. Some restaurants banned tipping and increased prices, but the movement toward no-tipping services has largely fizzled out 失败.

So, to increase employee wages without raising prices, more employers are succumbing to 屈服于 the temptations of tip creep and tipflation. However, many customers are frustrated 沮丧 because they feel they are being asked for too high of a tip too often. And, as our research emphasizes, tipping now seems to be more coercive 强制性的, less generous, and often completely disassociated from 脱离 service quality.

When it was established, the National Health Service (NHS) 英国国民保健制度 was visionary 有远见的: offering high-quality, timely care 及时护理 to meet the dominant needs 主要需求 of the population it served. Nearly 75 years on, with the UK facing very different health challenges, it is clear that the model is out of date 过时的.

From the life expectancy 预期寿命 to cancer and infant mortality rates 婴儿死亡率, we are lagging behind 落后于 many of our peers. With more than 6.8 million on waitlists 等待名单, healthcare is becoming increasingly inaccessible 难以获得 for those who cannot opt to 选择 pay for private treatment; and the cost of providing healthcare is increasingly squeezing 挤压 our investment in other public services. As demand for health care continues to grow, pressures on the workforce—which is already near breaking point 崩溃边缘—will only become more acute 严重.

Many of the answers to the crisis in the health and care are well rehearsed 充分讨论的. We need to be much better at reducing and diverting 转移 demand on health services, rather than simply managing it. Much more needs to be invested in communities and primary care 初级保健 to reduce our reliance on hospitals. And capacity in social care 社会护理 needs to be greater, to support the growing number of people living with long-term conditions 长期病症.

Yet despite two decades of strategies and a number of major health reforms, we have failed to make meaningful progress 有意义的进展 on any of these aims. That is why the Reform think tank 改革智库 is launching a new programme of work entitled "Reimagining health" "重新构想健康", supported by ten former health ministers. Together, we are calling for a much more open and honest conversation about the future of health in the UK and on an "urgent rethink" 紧急反思 of the hospital-centric model 以医院为中心的模式 we retain. This must begin with the question of how we maximize the health of the nation, rather than "fix" the NHS. It is estimated, for example, that healthcare accounts for only about 20% of health outcomes 健康结果. Much more important are the places we live, work and socialize 社交—yet there is no clear cross-government strategy 跨政府战略 for improving these social determinants of health 健康的社会决定因素. Worse, when policies like the national obesity strategy 国家肥胖战略 are scrapped 取消, taxpayers are left with the hefty price tag 高昂代价 of treating the illnesses, like diabetes 糖尿病, that result.

Reform wants to ask how power and resources should be distributed 分配 in our health system. What health functions should remain at the centre 中央, and what should be given to local leaders 地方领导人, often responsible for services that create health, and with a much better understanding of the needs of their populations?

Heat action plans 热浪行动计划, or HAPs, have been proliferating 激增 in India in the past few years. In general, an HAP spells out 详细说明 when and how officials should issue heat warnings 发布高温预警 and alert hospitals and other institutions. Nagpur's plan, for instance, calls for 要求 hospitals to set aside 留出 "cold wards" 冷病房 in the summer for treating heatstroke 中暑 patients, and advises builders to give construction laborers 建筑工人 a break from work on very hot days.

But implementation 实施 of existing HAPs has been uneven 不均衡的, according to a report from the Center for Policy Research 政策研究中心. Many lack adequate funding 充足资金, it found. And their triggering thresholds 触发阈值 often are not customized 定制 to the local climate. In some areas, high daytime temperatures alone might serve as an adequate trigger for alerts. But in other places, nighttime temperatures or humidity 湿度 might be as important a gauge 衡量标准 of risk as daytime highs.

Mumbai's April heat stroke deaths highlighted 凸显 the need for more nuanced 细致的 and localized warnings, researchers say. That day's high temperature of roughly 36°C was 1°C shy of 低于 the heat wave alert threshold for coastal cities 沿海城市 set by national meteorological authorities 国家气象部门. But the effects of the heat were amplified 放大 by humidity—an often neglected factor in heat alert systems—and the lack of shade 阴凉 at the late-morning outdoor ceremony.

To help improve HAPs, urban planner Kotharkar's team is working on a model plan 模型计划 that outlines best practices 最佳实践 and could be adapted to 适用于 local conditions. Among other things, she says, all cities should create a vulnerability map 脆弱性地图 to help focus responses on the populations most at risk 风险最高的人群.

Such mapping doesn't need to be complex, Kotharkar says. "A useful map can be created by looking at even a few key parameters 关键参数." For example, neighborhoods with a large elderly population or informal dwellings 非正式住宅 that cope poorly with heat could get special warnings or be bolstered 加强 with cooling centers 降温中心. The Nagpur project has already created a risk and vulnerability map, which enabled Kotharkar to tell officials which neighborhoods to focus on in the event of a heat wave this summer.

HAPs shouldn't just include short-term emergency responses 短期应急响应, researchers say, but also recommend medium- to long-term measures 中长期措施 that could make communities cooler. In Nagpur, for example, Kotharkar's team has been able to advise city officials about where to plant trees to provide shade. HAPs could also guide efforts to retrofit 改造 homes or modify building regulations 修改建筑法规. "Reducing deaths in an emergency is a good target to have, but it's the lowest target," says climate researcher Chandni Singh.

##### 新题型 Part B（41-45）

#### 题型说明

**小标题匹配题（7选5）**：从7个备选小标题（A-G）中为5个段落选择最合适的小标题，有2个多余选项。文章主题为"五步提出工作变革建议，确保被认真对待"。

#### 原文

**Five Steps to Suggesting a Change at Work That'll Actually Get Taken Seriously**

Everyone wants to be that person—the one who looks at the same information as everyone else, but who sees a fresh, innovative solution. However, it takes more than simply having a good idea. How you share it is as important as the idea itself.

Here's how to get support for your innovative ideas.

**41. ______**

Great ideas don't exist in a vacuum 真空. In other words, you can't just propose something once and expect it to be adopted. To see change, you need to champion 捍卫 your plan, sell its benefits. Moreover, you need to be willing to accept scrutiny 审查 and criticism, and be prepared to explain your innovation in different ways to different audiences.

**42. ______**

Sometimes it makes sense to go to your boss first. But other times it's useful to build a coalition 联盟 among your colleagues or other stakeholders. When this approach works, it works beautifully—because you're prepared for the stubborn boss's pushback with, "Actually, I've already connected with a few people in tech to discuss how much time this website update would require, and they're on board."

**43. ______**

One of the biggest barriers to getting support is when the idea's champion is seen as argumentative, defensive, or closed-minded. Because truthfully, no one likes a know-it-all. So, if someone disagrees with you, don't be indignant 愤慨的. Instead, fully listen to their concerns, try to understand their position, and incorporate their concerns (and possible solutions) into future discussions.

So, instead of saying, "Martha, our current slogan is confusing and should be updated," you could try, "Martha raises a great point that our current slogan has a long history for our stakeholders, but I wonder if we might be able to brainstorm a tagline that could build on that—and be clearer for new customers."

**44. ______**

New ideas are the grandchildren of old ones. In other words, don't throw old solutions under the bus 贬低 to make your improvement stand out. Remember that whatever the problem the old system solved—or has failed to solve in recent memory—it was a great idea at the time. Appreciating the older contributions as you suggest future innovations helps bolster 增强 the credibility of your idea.

**45. ______**

When pitching a new idea, it's important to use the language of abundance instead of the language of deficit. Instead of saying what is wrong, broken, or suboptimal 次优的, talk about what is right, fixable, or ideal. For example, try, "I can see lots of applications for this new approach" rather than, "This innovation is the only way." Be optimistic but realistic, and you will stand out.

#### 题目

从以下选项中为段落41-45选择最合适的小标题（A-G），其中两个为多余选项：

[A] Stay positive. 保持积极
[B] Respect the past. 尊重过去
[C] Use channels. 使用渠道
[D] Give it time. 给它时间
[E] Invite resistance. 邀请反对
[F] Be a salesman. 做一名推销员
[G] Be humble. 保持谦逊

---
##### 参考答案
41.F 42.C 43.G 44.B 45.A

#### 解析

**题型特点**：小标题匹配题（7选5），考查段落主旨概括能力。

**解题策略**：
1. 先读选项，标记核心动词（Stay/Respect/Use/Give/Invite/Be）
2. 回到段落，定位中心句（通常在段首或段尾）
3. 注意段落中关键词与选项词汇的同义复现

**逐段解析**：
- **41题 F**：段落核心"you need to champion your plan, sell its benefits"=你需要捍卫计划并推销其优点，"explain your innovation in different ways to different audiences"=向不同受众解释创新。对应F"Be a salesman"（做一名推销员）。
- **42题 C**：段落讲"go to your boss first"（先找上司）和"build a coalition among colleagues"（在同事中组建联盟），都是关于选择合适的沟通渠道。对应C"Use channels"（使用渠道）。
- **43题 G**：段落讲"don't be indignant"（不要愤慨），"listen to their concerns"（倾听担忧），"understand their position"（理解立场），体现谦逊开放态度。对应G"Be humble"（保持谦逊）。
- **44题 B**：段落核心"don't throw old solutions under the bus"（不要贬低旧方案），"it was a great idea at the time"（在当时是好主意），"appreciating the older contributions"（欣赏过去的贡献）。对应B"Respect the past"（尊重过去）。
- **45题 A**：段落讲"use the language of abundance instead of deficit"（用丰富的语言而非匮乏的语言），"talk about what is right, fixable, or ideal"（谈论正确的、可修复的或理想的），"Be optimistic but realistic"（保持乐观但现实）。对应A"Stay positive"（保持积极）。
