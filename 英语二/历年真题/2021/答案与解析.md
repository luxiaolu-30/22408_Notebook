#### 原文

It's not difficult to set targets for staff. It is much harder, (1), to understand their negative consequences. Most work-related behaviors have multiple components. (2) one and the others become distorted 扭曲的.

Travel on a London bus and you'll (3) see how this works with drivers. Watch people get on and show their tickets. Are they carefully inspected 检查? Never. Do people get on without paying? Of course! Are there inspectors to (4) that people have paid? Possibly, but very few. And people who run for the bus? They are (5). How about jumping lights? Buses do so almost as frequently as cyclists.

Why? Because the target is (6). People complained that buses were late and infrequent 班次少的. (7), the number of buses and bus lanes were increased, and drivers were (8) or punished according to the time they took. And drivers hit these targets. But they (9) hit cyclists. If the target was changed to (10), you would have more inspectors and more sensitive pricing. If the criterion changed to safety, you would get more (11) drivers who obeyed traffic laws. But both these criteria would be at the expense of time.

There is another (12): people became immensely inventive in hitting targets. Have you (13) that you can leave on a flight an hour late but still arrive on time? Tailwinds? Of course not! Airlines have simply changed the time a (14) is meant to take. A one-hour flight is now billed as a two-hour flight.

The (15) of the story is simple. Most jobs are multidimensional, with multiple criteria. Choose one criterion and you may well (16) others. Everything can be done faster and made cheaper, but there is a (17). Setting targets can and does have unforeseen negative consequences.

This is not an argument against target-setting. But it is an argument for exploring consequences first. All good targets should have multiple criteria (18) critical factors such as time, money, quality and customer feedback. The trick is not only to (19) just one or even two dimensions of the objective, but also to understand how to help people better (20) the objective.

#### 重点解析

- **第1题（B however）**：逻辑连词。前句"设定目标不难"与后句"理解其负面后果难得多"，not difficult与much harder语义相反，however表转折。

- **第2题（A Emphasize）**：逻辑关系。one和the others提示对比，and前后语义相反。"强调一个，其余则被扭曲"，Emphasize=强调，符合。

- **第4题（C check）**：词义辨析+语境。前文问"人们会逃票吗？当然！"，此处问"有检票员检查（check）人们是否买票吗？"，check与inspect同义复现。

- **第6题（A punctuality）**：逻辑推断。下文"司机根据所花时间奖惩"及"司机们达到了目标"，目标与时间相关，punctuality=准时，符合。

- **第8题（C rewarded）**：逻辑连词。or提示选择关系，or后punished（惩罚），则应选rewarded（奖励）与之构成对比，"或奖或罚"。

- **第10题（B revenue）**：语境推断。后文"更多检票员和更敏感的定价"与金钱有关，revenue=收益，符合。

- **第12题（B problem）**：逻辑关系。another提示与前文语义一致，前文讲设定目标的负面影响，problem=问题，呼应。

- **第15题（A moral）**：固定搭配。"the moral of the story"=故事的寓意，用于总结前文航空公司修改航班时长的故事。

- **第17题（D cost）**：逻辑连词。but提示前后语义相反，前句"可以更快更便宜"，后句"但有代价"，cost=代价，符合。

- **第20题（D achieve）**：动宾搭配。"实现目标"=achieve the objective，modify修改、review复习、present展示均不符。

Reskilling 再培训 is something that sounds like a buzzword 流行词 but is actually a requirement if we plan to have a future where a lot of would-be workers 准工人 do not get left behind 落后.

We know we are moving into a period where the jobs in demand 需求中的工作 will change rapidly, as will the requirements of the jobs that remain. Research by the WEF 世界经济论坛 detailed in the Harvard Business Review, finds that on average 42 per cent of the core skills "within job roles will change by 2022. That is a very short timeline, so we can only imagine what the changes will be further in the future.

The question of who should pay for reskilling is a thorny 棘手的 one. For individual companies, the temptation 诱惑 is always to let go of 解雇 workers whose skills are no longer in demand and replace them with those whose skills are. That does not always happen. AT&T is often given as the gold standard 黄金标准 of a company who decided to do a massive reskilling program rather than go with a fire-and-hire strategy 解雇再雇佣策略, ultimately retraining 18,000 employees. Prepandemic 疫情前, other companies including Amazon and Disney had also pledged 承诺 to create their own plans. When the skills mismatch 技能错配 is in the broader economy though, the focus usually turns to government to handle. Efforts in Canada and elsewhere have been arguably languid 懒散的 at best, and have given us a situation where we frequently hear of employers begging for workers even at times and in regions where unemployment is high.

With the pandemic, unemployment is very high indeed. In February, at 3.5 per cent and 5.5 per cent respectively 分别地, unemployment rates in Canada and the United States were at generational lows 几代人低点 and worker shortages were everywhere. As of May, those rates had spiked 飙升 up to 13.3 per cent and 13.7 per cent, and although many worker shortages had disappeared, not all had done so. In the medical field, to take an obvious example, the pandemic meant that there were still clear shortages of doctors, nurses and other medical personnel 医务人员.

Of course, it is not like you can take an unemployed waiter and train him to be a doctor in a few weeks, no matter who pays for it. But even if you cannot close that gap, maybe you can close others, and doing so would be to the benefit of all concerned 所有相关方. That seems to be the case in Sweden, where the pandemic kick-started 启动 a retraining program where business as well as government had a role.

Reskilling in this way would be challenging in a North American context 北美背景. You can easily imagine a chorus of 齐声 "you can't do that," because teachers or nurses or whoever have special skills, and using any support staff who has been quickly trained is bound to 一定会 end in disaster. Maybe. Or maybe it is something that can work well in Sweden, with its history of co-operation between business, labour and government, but not in North America where our history is very different. Then again, maybe it is akin to 类似于 wartime, when extraordinary things take place, but it is business as usual 一切照旧 after the fact. And yet, as in war the pandemic is teaching us that many things, including rapid reskilling, can be done if there is a will to do them. In any case, Sweden's workforce is now more skilled, in more things, and more flexible than it was before.

Of course, reskilling programs, whether for pandemic needs or the post-pandemic world, are expensive and at a time when everyone's budgets are lean 拮据 this may not be the time to implement them. Then again, extending income support programs to get us through the next months is expensive, too, to say nothing of 更不用说 the cost of having a swath of 一大批 long-term unemployed in the post-COVID years. Given that, perhaps we should think hard about whether the pandemic can jump-start us to a place where reskilling becomes much more than a buzzword.

##### Text1（21-25）

#### 长难句1

> Reskilling is something that sounds like a buzzword but is actually a requirement if we plan to have a future where a lot of would-be workers do not get left behind.

##### 句子拆分

主干：**Reskilling is something**
- `that sounds like a buzzword but is actually a requirement`：that定语从句，修饰something，but连接两个并列谓语
- `if we plan to have a future where a lot of would-be workers do not get left behind`：if条件状语从句
  - `where a lot of would-be workers do not get left behind`：where定语从句，修饰future

##### 生词

reskilling：再培训；buzzword：流行词、时髦术语；requirement：要求、必要条件；plan to：计划；would-be workers：准工人、未来工人；get left behind：被落下、落后

##### 翻译

再培训听起来像是一个流行词，但实际上如果我们计划拥有一个许多准工人不会被落下的未来，它就是一个必要条件。

##### 考点

**that定语从句+but并列谓语+if条件状语从句+where定语从句**；开篇点题——再培训是必要的，不是流行词。

With the global population predicted to hit close to 10 billion by 2050, and forecasts that agricultural production in some regions will need to nearly double to keep pace 跟上步伐, food security 粮食安全 is increasingly making headlines 成为头条新闻. In the UK, it has become a big talking point 热门话题 recently too, for a rather particular reason: Brexit 英国脱欧.

Brexit is seen by some as an opportunity to reverse 逆转 a recent trend towards the UK importing food. The country produces only about 60 percent of the food it eats, down from almost three-quarters in the late 1980s. A move back to self-sufficiency 自给自足, the argument goes, would boost the farming industry, political sovereignty 政治主权 and even the nation's health. Sounds great—but how feasible 可行的 is this vision?

According to a report on UK food production from the University of Leeds, UK, 85 per cent of the country's total land area is associated with 与…相关 meat and dairy production 肉类和奶制品生产. That supplies 80 per cent of what is consumed, so even covering the whole country in livestock farms 畜牧场 wouldn't allow us to cover all our meat and dairy needs.

There are many caveats 警告 to those figures, but they are still grave 严峻的. To become much more self-sufficient, the UK would need to drastically reduce 大幅减少 its consumption of animal foods, and probably also farm more intensively 更集约化地耕作—meaning fewer green fields, and more factory-style production 工厂式生产.

But switching to 转向 a mainly plant-based diet 植物性饮食 wouldn't help. There is a good reason why the UK is dominated by 以…为主 animal husbandry 畜牧业: most of its terrain 地形 doesn't have the right soil or climate to grow crops on a commercial basis 商业化基础. Just 25 percent of the country's land is suitable for crop-growing 作物种植, most of which is already occupied by arable fields 可耕地. Even if we converted 转换 all the suitable land to fields of fruit and veg—which would involve taking out all the nature reserves 自然保护区 and removing thousands of people from their homes—we would achieve only a 30 percent boost in crop production.

Just 23 percent of the fruit and vegetables consumed in the UK are currently home-grown 国产的, so even with the most extreme measures we could meet only 30 percent of our fresh produce 新鲜农产品 needs. That is before we look for the space to grow the grains, sugars, seeds and oils that provide us with the vast bulk of 绝大部分 our current calorie intake 卡路里摄入.

When Microsoft bought task management app 任务管理应用 Wunderlist and mobile calendar 移动日历 Sunrise in 2015, it picked up 获得 two newcomers 新公司 that were attracting considerable buzz 相当大的关注 in Silicon Valley 硅谷. Both apps, however, were later scrapped 废弃, after Microsoft said it had used their best features in its own products. Their teams of engineers stayed on 留下, making them two of the many "acqui-hires 人才收购" that the biggest companies have used to feed their insatiable hunger 无法满足的渴望 for tech talent 科技人才.

To Microsoft's critics 批评者, the fates of Wunderlist and Sunrise are examples of a remorseless drive 无情的驱动力 by Big Tech to chew up 吞噬 any innovative companies that lie in their path. Microsoft declined to comment 拒绝置评.

Like other start-up investors, Mr Arnold's own business often depends on 依赖 selling start-ups to larger tech companies, though he admits to mixed feelings 复杂感受 about the result: "I think these things are good for me, if I put my selfish hat on 从自私角度. The US Federal Trade Commission 美国联邦贸易委员会 says it wants to find the answer to that question. This week, it asked the five most valuable US tech companies for information about their many small acquisitions 小型收购 over the past decade. Although only a research project at this stage, the request has raised the prospect 前景 of regulators wading into 介入 early-stage tech markets that until now have been beyond their reach 超出其范围.

Given their combined market value of more than $5.5tn, rifling through 仔细检查 such small deals—many of them much less prominent 不那么显眼 than Wunderlist and Sunrise—might seem beside the point 离题. However, critics say that the big companies use such deals to buy their most threatening potential competitors before their businesses have a chance to gain momentum 获得势头, in some cases as part of a "buy and kill" tactic 策略 to simply close them down.

We're fairly good at judging people based on first impressions 第一印象, thin slices of experience 经验薄片 ranging from a glimpse of a photo to a five-minute interaction, and deliberation 深思熟虑 can be not only extraneous 无关的 but intrusive 打扰的. In one study of the ability she dubbed 称为 "thin slicing," the late psychologist 已故心理学家 Nalini Ambady asked participants to watch silent 10-second video clips 视频片段 of professors and to rate the instructor's overall effectiveness 整体有效性. Their ratings correlated strongly with 与…强相关 students' end-of-semester ratings. Another set of participants had to count backward 倒数 from 1,000 by nines as they watched the clips, occupying their conscious working memory 有意识工作记忆. Their ratings were just as accurate, demonstrating the intuitive nature 直觉本质 of the social processing 社会处理.

Critically 关键地, another group was asked to spend a minute writing down reasons for their judgment, before giving the rating. Accuracy dropped dramatically 急剧下降. Ambady suspected that deliberation focused them on vivid but misleading cues 生动但误导的线索, such as certain gestures or utterances 话语, rather than letting the complex interplay 复杂相互作用 of subtle signals 微妙信号 form a holistic impression 整体印象. She found similar interference 干扰 when participants watched 15-second clips of pairs of people and judged whether they were strangers, friends, or dating partners 约会对象.

Other research shows we're better at detecting deception 检测欺骗 and sexual orientation 性取向 from thin slices when we rely on intuition 直觉 instead of reflection 反思. "It's as if you're driving a stick shift 手动挡," says Judith Hall, a psychologist at Northeastern University, "and if you start thinking about it too much, you can't remember what you're doing. But if you go on automatic pilot 自动驾驶, you're fine. Much of our social life is like that."

Thinking too much can also harm our ability to form preferences 形成偏好. College students' ratings of strawberry jams 草莓酱 and college courses aligned better with 与…更一致 experts' opinions when the students weren't asked to analyze their rationale 分析理由. And people made car-buying decisions that were both objectively better 客观上更好 and more personally satisfying 更个人满意 when asked to focus on their feelings rather than on details, but only if the decision was complex—when they had a lot of information to process.

Intuition's special powers are unleashed 释放 only in certain circumstances. In one study, participants completed a battery of 一组 eight tasks, including four that tapped reflective thinking 反思思维 (discerning rules 辨别规则, comprehending vocabulary 理解词汇) and four that tapped intuition and creativity (generating new products or figures of speech 修辞). Then they rated the degree to which they had used intuition ("gut feelings 直觉," "hunches 预感," "my heart"). Use of their gut hurt their performance on the first four tasks, as expected, and helped them on the rest. Sometimes the heart is smarter than the head.

**and连接并列谓语+as expected插入语+比较级**；40题B选项的依据——直觉可能影响反思型任务（Intuition may affect reflective tasks = Use of their gut hurt their performance on the first four tasks, which tapped reflective thinking）。

##### Text4（36-40）

> 原文主题：我们擅长基于第一印象判断人，深思熟虑反而有害；安巴迪研究发现10秒视频评分与学期末评分强相关，工作记忆被占据时仍准确（直觉本质），但要求写下理由时准确性下降（专注于误导性细节）；其他研究显示直觉比反思更能检测欺骗和性取向；霍尔用开手动挡比喻说明想太多会分心；想太多也损害形成偏好的能力，复杂决定时专注感受更好；最后研究显示直觉损害反思型任务但帮助直觉型任务，有时心比头聪明

#### 36. Nalini Ambady's study deals with ______.

[A] instructor student interaction [B] the power of people's memory [C] the reliability of first impressions [D] people's ability to influence others

✅答案：**C** 定位：第一段。`We're fairly good at judging people based on first impressions, thin slices of experience`（我们相当擅长基于第一印象判断人——经验薄片）+ `In one study of the ability she dubbed "thin slicing"`（在一项她称为"薄片切割"能力的研究中）。说明安巴迪的研究涉及第一印象的可靠性。

- A：错误。instructor student interaction（师生互动）是研究材料，不是研究主题。
- B：错误。the power of people's memory（人们记忆的力量）无中生有。
- C：正确。第一印象的可靠性。judging people based on first impressions + thin slicing + ratings correlated with end-of-semester ratings = the reliability of first impressions。
- D：错误。people's ability to influence others（人们影响他人的能力）无中生有。

#### 37. In Ambady's study, rating accuracy dropped when participants ______.

[A] gave the rating in limited time [B] focused on specific details [C] watched shorter video clips [D] discussed with one another

✅答案：**B** 定位：第二段。`another group was asked to spend a minute writing down reasons for their judgment, before giving the rating. Accuracy dropped dramatically. Ambady suspected that deliberation focused them on vivid but misleading cues, such as certain gestures or utterances`（另一组被要求在评分前花一分钟写下判断理由。准确性急剧下降。安巴迪认为，深思熟虑把他们集中在生动但误导的线索上，如某些手势或话语）。说明当参与者专注于具体细节时，评分准确性下降。

- A：错误。gave the rating in limited time（在有限时间内评分）无中生有。
- B：正确。专注于具体细节。focused them on vivid but misleading cues, such as certain gestures or utterances = focused on specific details。
- C：错误。watched shorter video clips（观看更短的视频片段）与原文相反。另一组看15秒片段，不是更短。
- D：错误。discussed with one another（互相讨论）无中生有。

#### 38. Judith Hall mentions driving to show that ______.

[A] memory can be selective [B] reflection can be distracting [C] social skills must be cultivated [D] deception is difficult to detect

✅答案：**B** 定位：第三段。`"It's as if you're driving a stick shift, and if you start thinking about it too much, you can't remember what you're doing. But if you go on automatic pilot, you're fine. Much of our social life is like that."`（"这就好像你在开手动挡车，如果你开始想得太多，你就记不起自己在做什么。但如果你自动驾驶，你就没事。我们的社会生活大多如此。"）说明霍尔提到开车是为了说明反思可能让人分心。

- A：错误。memory can be selective（记忆可以是选择性的）偷换概念。是想太多记不起在做什么，不是记忆选择性。
- B：正确。反思可能让人分心。if you start thinking about it too much, you can't remember what you're doing = reflection can be distracting。
- C：错误。social skills must be cultivated（社交技能必须培养）无中生有。
- D：错误。deception is difficult to detect（欺骗难以检测）与原文相反。直觉更能检测欺骗。

#### 39. When you are making complex decisions, it is advisable to ______.

[A] follow your feelings [B] list your preferences [C] seek expert advice [D] collect enough data

✅答案：**A** 定位：第四段。`people made car-buying decisions that were both objectively better and more personally satisfying when asked to focus on their feelings rather than on details, but only if the decision was complex`（当被要求专注于感受而不是细节时，人们做出的购车决定既客观上更好，也更个人满意，但只有当决定复杂时）。说明做复杂决定时，建议跟随感受。

- A：正确。跟随你的感受。focus on their feelings rather than on details + complex decisions = follow your feelings。
- B：错误。list your preferences（列出你的偏好）无中生有。
- C：错误。seek expert advice（寻求专家建议）偷换概念。学生评分与专家意见更一致是不分析理由时，不是做决定时寻求专家建议。
- D：错误。collect enough data（收集足够数据）与原文相反。复杂决定时有大量信息要处理，专注感受更好，不是收集更多数据。

#### 40. What can we learn from the last paragraph?

##### 本篇命题规律小结（英二阅读）

1. 心理/科普类文章：关注**观点引入（第一印象准确，深思有害）→实验1（10秒视频评分与学期末评分相关）→实验2（工作记忆被占据仍准确=直觉本质）→实验3（写下理由准确性下降=专注误导细节）→其他研究（直觉检测欺骗更好）→比喻（开手动挡说明想太多分心）→应用（复杂决定专注感受更好）→限定（直觉只在特定情境有效，损害反思任务但帮助直觉任务）**的结构
2. 主旨题：定位到**开篇观点+研究主题**（first impressions, thin slicing）
3. 细节题：定位到**实验结果+原因解释**（deliberation focused them on vivid but misleading cues）
4. 例证题：定位到**人物直接引语+比喻**（driving a stick shift, thinking too much → can't remember）
5. 推理题：定位到**最后一段实验分类+结果**（gut hurt reflective tasks, helped intuitive tasks）

##### 新题型 Part B（41-45）

#### 题型说明

**小标题匹配题（7选5）**：从7个备选小标题（A-G）中为5个段落选择最合适的小标题，有2个多余选项。文章主题为"如何与比你更有权力的人意见相左"。

#### 原文

**How to Disagree with Someone More Powerful than You**

Your boss proposes a new initiative you think won't work. Your senior colleague outlines a project timeline you think is unrealistic. What do you say when you disagree with someone who has more power than you do? How do you decide whether it's worth speaking up? And if you do, what exactly should you say? Here's how to disagree with someone more powerful than you.

**41. ______**

You may decide it's best to hold off on voicing your opinion 暂缓发表意见. Maybe you haven't finished thinking the problem through, the whole discussion was a surprise to you, or you want to get a clearer sense of what the group thinks. If you think other people are going to disagree too, you might want to gather your army first. People can contribute experience or information to your thinking—all the things that would make the disagreement stronger or more valid. It's also a good idea to delay the conversation if you're in a meeting or other public space. Discussing the issue in private will make the powerful person feel less threatened.

**42. ______**

Before you share your thoughts, think about what the powerful person cares about—it may be the credibility of their team or getting a project done on time. You're more likely to be heard if you can connect your disagreement to a higher purpose. When you do speak up, don't assume the link will be clear. You'll want to state it overtly 公开地, contextualizing your statements so that you're seen not as a disagreeable underling 下属 but as a colleague who's trying to advance a shared goal. The discussion will then become more like a chess game than a boxing match.

**43. ______**

This step may sound overly deferential 恭顺的, but it's a smart way to give the powerful person psychological safety and control. You can say something like, "I know we seem to be moving toward a first-quarter commitment here. I have reasons to think that won't work. I'd like to lay out my reasoning. Would that be OK?" This gives the person a choice, allowing them to verbally opt in. And, assuming they say yes, it will make you feel more confident about voicing your disagreement.

**44. ______**

You might feel your heart racing or your face turning red, but do whatever you can to remain neutral in both your words and actions. When your body language communicates reluctance or anxiety, it undercuts the message. It sends a mixed message, and your counterpart gets to choose what to read. Deep breaths can help, as can speaking more slowly and deliberately. When we feel panicky we tend to talk louder and faster. Simply slowing the pace and talking in an even tone helps the other person calm down and does the same for you. It also makes you seem confident, even if you aren't.

**45. ______**

Emphasize that you're offering your opinion, not gospel truth 圣经真理. It may be a well-informed, well-researched opinion, but it's still an opinion, so talk tentatively and slightly understate your confidence. Instead of saying something like, "If we set an end-of-quarter deadline, we'll never make it," say, "This is just my opinion, but I don't see how we will make that deadline." Having asserted your position (as a position, not as a fact) demonstrates equal curiosity about other views. Remind the person that this is your point of view, and then invite critique. Be open to hearing other opinions.

#### 题目

从以下选项中为段落41-45选择最合适的小标题（A-G），其中两个为多余选项：

[A] Stay calm. 保持冷静
[B] Stay humble. 保持谦逊
[C] Don't make judgments. 不要妄下判断
[D] Be realistic about the risks. 务实评估风险
[E] Decide whether to wait. 决定是否等待
[F] Ask permission to disagree. 请求允许发表异议
[G] Identify a shared goal. 找到共同目标

---
##### 参考答案
41.E 42.G 43.F 44.A 45.B

#### 解析

**题型特点**：小标题匹配题（7选5），考查段落主旨概括能力。选项均为祈使句短语，需与段落核心建议对应。

**解题策略**：
1. 先读选项，标记核心动词（Decide/Identify/Ask/Stay/Don't/Be）
2. 回到段落，定位中心句（通常在段首或段尾）
3. 注意段落中关键词与选项词汇的同义复现

**逐段解析**：
- **41题 E**：段落首句"hold off on voicing your opinion"=暂缓发表意见，"delay the conversation"=延迟对话，均与E"Decide whether to wait"（决定是否等待）对应。
- **42题 G**：段落核心词"shared goal"（共同目标）在文中直接出现，"higher purpose"（更高目标）也是线索，对应G"Identify a shared goal"。
- **43题 F**：段落举例"Would that be OK?"（可以吗？）体现"请求许可"，"gives the person a choice"给人选择权，对应F"Ask permission to disagree"。
- **44题 A**：段落"heart racing"和"face turning red"表明紧张，but后"remain neutral"=保持中立，"help the other person calm down"=帮助冷静，对应A"Stay calm"。
- **45题 B**：段落"not gospel truth"不是绝对真理，"talk tentatively"试探性地说，"invite critique"邀请批评，"Be open to hearing other opinions"开放听取他人意见，均体现谦逊态度，对应B"Stay humble"。
